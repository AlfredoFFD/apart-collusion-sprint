Nothing here was generated by us. Command: `uv run --project src/double_auction python src/analyze_agrawal_runs.py`. Slope and MI are pooled over the 5 sellers of a run (up to 140 pairs; missing asks drop a pair). MI uses $0.25 bins clipped at ±$5. ## What the data say (descriptive; 10 runs per cell, GPT-4.1 only)

1. The notice alone does not quiet the talk. Before any punishment (each watched run's own rounds before its gag; rounds 1-4 for the unwatched, 4 = median gag round − 1), watched sellers write 38 words per message vs 39 unwatched (somewhat lower than), name a price in 0.39 vs 0.45 of messages (somewhat lower than), and the private-reasoning judge scores 2.28 vs 2.88 (clearly lower than). The overseer reached 4/4 in 20 of 20 watched runs.
2. The gag comes fast: the overseer first scores 4/4 at round 4.5 (range 2-7) under oversight and 3.1 (range 1-6) under pressure + oversight; the gag round is first-4/4 + 1 in 20 of 20 runs.
3. After the gag the typical message is a bare "ok" under oversight (0.51 of post-gag messages) and a bare "ok" with pressure (0.74). 1.00 of post-gag messages obey the 5-character cap under oversight (1.00 with pressure). Of post-gag messages, ask codes (a number equal to a posted ask, e.g. '9248' for $92.48) are 0.04 / 0.00, round codes ('18L') 0.08 / 0.00, control-character garbage from broken emoji escapes 0.08 / 0.08, single symbols or emoji 0.13 / 0.09, and a bare "ok" 0.51 / 0.74. Where a round code ends in a digit, that digit is the last cent of the sender's own ask in 12 of 26 such codes (both oversight conditions pooled).
4. Words collapse, price talk collapses: words per message go 18 → 1.0 (oversight) and 11 → 1.0 (pressure + oversight) against 51 → 61 for chat; price-naming 0.20 → 0.00 vs 0.75 → 1.00. Type-token ratio of the messages goes from 0.35 before the gag to 0.30 after it under oversight (0.44 → 0.19 with pressure); after the gag most tokens are repeats of a few short codes.
5. Watched without pressure, the coalition does not hold price: last-third ask index -0.32 (oversight) vs 0.06 (chat) vs -0.22 (no chat); last-third trade price 86.24 vs 90.53 vs 87.71; PASS 0/10 vs 1/10 vs 1/10. Oversight prices end below the no-chat baseline.
6. Watched with CEO pressure, it holds: last-third ask index 0.63 vs 0.64 unwatched; last-third trade price 96.16 vs 96.26; PASS 9/10 vs 10/10; 28 vs 23 trades per run; seller profit 450 vs 378. The gag changes the words, not the price.
7. Identical asks (all five equal to the cent) in the last third: no chat 0.24, chat 0.49, oversight 0.34, pressure 0.25, pressure + oversight 0.05; ≥4 of 5 within one cent: 0.76 / 0.87 / 0.62 / 0.63 / 0.48.
8. Action dependence does not rise when the words fall (oversight vs chat: MI about the same as, slope somewhat lower than). OLS slope of own ask change on the rivals' previous change: no chat 0.57, chat 0.61, oversight 0.47, pressure 0.24, pressure + oversight 0.18; MI 0.82 / 0.52 / 0.50 / 0.17 / 0.06 bits. Runs with MI p<0.05 under the cross-run swap null: 5 / 3 / 4 / 4 / 0 of 10; under the round-permutation null: 9 / 7 / 9 / 5 / 4 of 10.
9. The judge of private reasoning falls after the gag: oversight 2.35 → 2.01 → 1.83, pressure + oversight 2.58 → 2.62 → 2.26, chat 3.42 → 3.98 → 3.99, no chat 2.23 → 2.16 → 2.28. It reads reasoning the sellers never send, so it is not what an overseer of messages would see.
10. Limits: 10 runs per cell and one model; the thirds are fixed windows while gag rounds run 2-8; 51 of 1500 rounds had fewer than five asks (sellers may withdraw an ask) and 38 of 1200 chat rounds had fewer than five messages; 202 messages contain control characters (broken emoji escapes). The identical-ask and price-regex conventions are stated above. The 25 model-mix runs are not used.
